The repeated invention of agriculture was a crucial development in human evolution, creating unrivaled population growth, and expansion of human populations. The connection of demographic migration with cultural innovations of agriculture naturally point to the correlated movement of culture and genes, which has garnered some statistical support on both global (Cavalli-Sforza), and local scales (Bantu). Closer inspection, however, reveals that the relationship between linguistic, and genetics expansion is complex (Stoneking et al. 2022). No region is that more the case than within the island of New Guinea.

Evidence of agricultural activity in New Guinea purportedly beings 10,000 years ago in the Kuk Swamp, found in the Western Highlands province of modern-day Papua New Guinea (Denham). Genetic evidence shows that effective population sizes take off in Southern Highlands, Chimbu, and Madang, around 10,000 years ago (Bergstrom, 2017). This Western end of the New Guinea Central Corridella, where the Kuk Swamp is also located, is also home to the deepest clades of Trans-New Guinea (TNG) languages, the largest languages family of New Guinea, and therefore the most likely candidate for the families origin (Pawley, XXX; Schapper, 2017). These basic facts leave a tantilizingly simple conclusion that agriculture was the catalyst for the co-evolution of demographic and linguistic expansion approximately 10kya. However, since dating the origin of TNG, or its sub-families, is a complex task - due to a combination of theoretical constraints, understudied languages, and an ever encroaching threat of language endangerment - support for this co-evolutionary process is largely circumstantial<better word than this>.

Phylogeny

We use BEAST v2.7.4 to infer the genealogical relationships between our set of 561 mtDNA. We use the BEAST package bModeltest to assess the most appropriate Substitution model. bModeltest will switch between all Transistion and transversion models, providing the model average across the runs and means we do not need to commit to a particular substitution model.

We use use a Coalescent Extended Bayesian Skyline Population history model (EBSP). The EBSP model leverages coalescent theory to account for fluctuating population size in the phylogenetic model, at multiple loci (Heled and Drummond 2008). The sparsity of historical knowledge in Southern New Guinea means we do not have a good understanding of historic changes in population size. We opt for using a model that will allow for a fluctuating population, rather than assuming that it did not fluctuate. We adjust the factor rate of the tree prior to 0.5, and change the population prior mean to an improper prior of 1/x, as suggested in Heled and Drummond (2008). We use a strict clock mutation. We determine mtDNA mutations rate of 2.4065 x 10-8 by taking the mean value from studies calibrating the human mitochondrial clock Fu et al. (2013).

In addition we imposed tiered monophyletic groupings for the M and N haplogroups, and then for E and Q haplogroups the within the M haplogroup and P within the N haplogroup.

Estimating the heritage of traits

To estimate the ‘channels of language’, we calculate the probability of two individuals sharing a language within a genetic clade, across the phylogenetic history. This calculation follow from (Lansing et al. 2017) and comes in the form:

\[ P(i_l,j_l)|i_{g(t)}, j_{g(t)}) = \frac{P(i_l, j_l \bigcap i_{g(t)}, j_{g(t)})}{P(i_{g(t)}, j_{g(t)}} = \frac{\sum_{g=1}^{c_t}(\sum_{i,j\neq i}1_{cos(l_ig, l_jg) > 0})}{\sum_{g=1}^{c_t}n_g(n_g - 1) / 2} \]

\(c_t\) is the number of genetic clades at any point in history, for t generations. We assume 25 year generation times. \(l_{i_g}\) and \(l_{j_g}\) are the languages or language families spoken by individuals within genetic clade \(g\). \(1_{cos(l_{i_g}, l_{i_g}) > 0}\) is 1 if the ith and jth individual share a language, and zero otherwise. \(n_g\) is the number of individuals in \(g\).
